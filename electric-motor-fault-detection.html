Electric Motor Fault Detection

Connecting motor modeling, vibration analysis, and machine learning to investigate mechanical faults.

Objective

Develop a vibration-based workflow for distinguishing healthy and faulty motor operation, with simulation and experimental validation as complementary parts of the capstone.

My contribution

I lead the machine-learning and code work, applying MATLAB signal processing and feature extraction. My capstone work also includes developing and validating a Simulink motor model and integrating vibration sensing with a microcontroller.

Methodology

  1. Inspect three-channel vibration records stored in MATLAB files.
  2. Remove the mean before FFT analysis and examine single-sided amplitude spectra.
  3. Extract crest factor, peak amplitude, kurtosis, dominant frequency, standard deviation, and RMS for each axis.
  4. Train a KNN classifier with a 70/30 holdout split and five-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter optimization within the training set.
  5. Evaluate held-out predictions using a confusion matrix, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 with Faulty as the positive class.
  6. Explore FFT coefficient retention and inverse-FFT reconstruction using RMSE and normalized RMSE.

Results & current status

The uploaded MATLAB script implements the feature extraction, classification, evaluation, and FFT reconstruction workflow. No independently reproduced accuracy or field-validation result is presented here.

Ongoing work

Simulink model validation, sensor integration, and evaluation on experimental motor data remain ongoing. Binary healthy/faulty classification does not yet establish reliable identification of individual fault types.

Scope & limitations

The script assumes a 1,000 Hz sampling frequency; that value needs confirmation against the dataset metadata. Random record splitting may overestimate generalization if records share a machine or acquisition run. A machine- or run-separated evaluation is a next validation step.
