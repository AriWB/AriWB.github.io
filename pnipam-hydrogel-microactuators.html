pNIPAM Hydrogel Microactuators

Fabricating thermoresponsive microstructures and studying their potential for soft actuation in microfluidics.

Objective

Investigate how temperature-driven swelling and shrinking of pNIPAM hydrogel microstructures can support soft actuation and microfluidic flow control.

My contribution

I prepared pNIPAM hydrogel resin, fabricated custom chips for two-photon direct laser writing, and fabricated hydrogel microstructures. I measured thermoresponsive behavior and examined how size, structure, and material properties influence the response.

Methodology

  1. Prepare hydrogel resin and experimental substrates.
  2. Fabricate microstructures with two-photon direct laser writing.
  3. Observe and measure temperature-dependent changes.
  4. Study the influence of structure and material properties on microactuator behavior.

Results & current status

The resume documents resin preparation, chip fabrication, hydrogel microstructure fabrication, and measurements of thermoresponsive behavior. Quantitative response-time, force, and flow-control performance are not presented.

Ongoing work

Application of these microstructures as soft microactuators and investigation of their performance remain ongoing research.

Scope & limitations

Detailed fabrication recipes, processing settings, unpublished microscopy, and raw laboratory data are excluded from this overview.

Source basis

September 2026 resume. Uploaded pNIPAM notes summarize literature and are not treated as original experimental results.
